## PART 3: DELIVERABLE POSITIONING ANALYSIS

### Option A: Feature-Focused
```
"You receive a professional report showing:
• What your decision depends on
• How your situation compares to similar situations
• Where your decision sits relative to what is typical"
```

**Strengths:**
- Bullet format is scannable
- Three clear benefits listed
- "What it depends on" is immediately clear
- Comparison element adds value

**Weaknesses:**
- Doesn't explain what "Commitment Pressure" is
- Doesn't show the deliverable format
- "Relative to what is typical" is vague

---

### Option B: Scope-Focused
```
"You receive a standardized assessment of the conditions supporting your decision."
```

**Strengths:**
- Single, clear statement
- "Standardized assessment" is credible
- "Conditions supporting" is specific

**Weaknesses:**
- Doesn't explain what you get (report? number? classification?)
- Doesn't mention comparison element
- "Assessment" is vague (written? verbal? data?)

---

### Option C: Classification-Focused
```
"You receive an independent classification of how dependent your decision is 
on things continuing to go right."
```

**Strengths:**
- "Independent" adds credibility
- Clear measurement ("how dependent")
- "Things continuing" is customer-language

**Weaknesses:**
- Doesn't explain format (report?)
- Doesn't mention comparison element
- Doesn't explain what the classification means
- "Things continuing to go right" is poetic but imprecise

---

### Option D (Recommended): Hybrid Approach
```
"You receive a professional report that:

1. Shows what your decision depends on
2. Classifies how dependent your decision is (Low, Moderate, Elevated, High, or Critical)
3. Compares your situation to similar situations
4. Explains what you should monitor going forward"
```

**Strengths:**
- Deliverable format is clear (report)
- Four distinct benefits, each clear
- Includes the classification with visible options
- Includes comparison element
- Includes actionable element (what to monitor)
- Addresses multiple buyer questions

**Weaknesses:**
- Slightly longer (but still scannable)
- Introduces classification name early

---

### Ranking

**Best to Worst:**
1. **Option D** (Hybrid) — Clearest, most comprehensive, highest conversion potential
2. **Option A** (Feature-Focused) — Good, but vague on format and classification
3. **Option C** (Classification-Focused) — Good on measurement, weak on format and comparison
4. **Option B** (Scope-Focused) — Too vague on deliverable

## PART 5: LANDING PAGE ARCHITECTURE AUDIT

### Current Assumed Order

1. Hero
2. What You Get
3. Why It Matters
4. Decision Types
5. Sample Report
6. How It Works
7. Decision Check™
8. Commitment Pressure™
9. What It Is / Is Not
10. CTA

---

### Analysis: Is This Optimal?

**Problems with current order:**
1. Sample Report comes too late (by step 5, understanding is already made)
2. Decision Check™ and Commitment Pressure™ come too late (step 7-8)
3. "What It Is / Is Not" comes after customer may have already decided
4. Decision Types (step 4) may be unnecessary at top level

---

### Optimal Order (Recommended)

**Phase 1: Clarity (Why and What)**
1. **Hero** — What the problem is and why now
2. **What You Actually Get** — The deliverable clearly explained (using Option D)
3. **Sample Report** — Proof of concept (show real output)

**Phase 2: Context (Why This Matters)**
4. **Why It Matters** — Stakes and relevance
5. **Decision Types** — "This applies to you if..."

**Phase 3: Understanding (How It Works)**
6. **How It Works** — Three-step process
7. **Decision Check™** — Product name and role
8. **Commitment Pressure™** — Classification system (after understanding established)

**Phase 4: Differentiation (Why This vs. Alternatives)**
9. **What It Is / Is Not** — Clear differentiation
10. **Methodology** — For detail-oriented visitors (optional)

**Phase 5: Action**
11. **CTA** — Final conversion

---

### Why This Order Is Better

**Why moving sample report to #3:**
- Proof of concept should come before abstract explanation
- Visitor sees what they get before understanding how/why
- "Seeing is believing" — real output creates trust

**Why moving Decision Check™ earlier:**
- Product name should come with understanding, not after
- Customer should know what they're buying before learning about Commitment Pressure™

**Why moving Commitment Pressure™ later:**
- Classification system is secondary to what they actually get
- Introducing it too early confuses understanding
- Better to show output first, then explain the measurement name
