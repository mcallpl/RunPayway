# PART 1: DO MEANINGFUL ARCHETYPES ACTUALLY EXIST?

## Defining Archetype Criteria

An archetype is a recurring pattern with:
1. **Consistent measurement profile** (similar measurements across different people)
2. **Structural similarity** (income works the same way)
3. **Predictable characteristics** (what you know about one member predicts others)
4. **Distinct boundaries** (clear differentiation from other archetypes)
5. **Meaningful differences** (different archetypes face different structural realities)

---

# PART 4: WOULD ARCHETYPES STRENGTHEN DECISION CHECK™?

## Testing Decision Relevance of Archetypes

**Hypothesis: Different archetypes face different structural realities for decisions.**

### Decision: Home Purchase

**Employment Dominant Archetype (Physician)**
- Structural reality: Very stable debt-service capacity, single-employer risk
- Decision implication: Strong qualification foundation, but employment-contingent

**Stable Base With Earned Overlay (Software Sales)**
- Structural reality: Stable base + moderate earned volatility
- Decision implication: Good qualification; base alone supports payment; earned is upside

**Multi-Component Hybrid (Financial Advisor)**
- Structural reality: Multiple income types, mixed labor/passive
- Decision implication: Complex qualification (averaging rules), but diversified

**Transaction Dominant (Real Estate Agent)**
- Structural reality: No stable floor, extreme variability
- Decision implication: Challenging qualification; requires 2-year history; high scrutiny

**Verdict: Different archetypes have fundamentally different decision implications.**

"For customers in the 'Stable Base With Earned Overlay' archetype seeking home purchase, structural characteristic X is typically relevant because..."

This is decision-relevant without being advice.

## Verdict on Strengthening Decision Check™: **YES, Significantly**

- Archetype-specific structural guidance is non-prescriptive and decision-relevant

# PART 5: WOULD ARCHETYPES BECOME A STRONGER MOAT THAN DECISION RELEVANCE ANALYSIS™?

## Comparing Moats

### Decision Relevance Analysis™ Moat
- Strength: Methodology-based (how to analyze)
- Replicability: 8-12 weeks for competitors to reverse-engineer
- Defensibility: Medium (framework is logical, auditable)
- Institutional adoption: Possible but requires standardization
- Long-term moat: Only if becomes industry standard (5-10 year timeline)

### Archetypes Moat
- Strength: Pattern-recognition-based (what to call the patterns)
- Replicability: 4-8 weeks for competitors to identify similar patterns
- Defensibility: HIGHER than Decision Relevance Analysis™ if archetypes are named and standardized
- Institutional adoption: HIGHER (easier to adopt named patterns than methodology)
- Long-term moat: YES if archetypes become category standard (becomes THE language of income structure)

---

## Why Archetypes Are Stronger Moat

**1. Naming creates stickiness**
- "Stable Base With Earned Overlay" is memorable and communicable
- Competitors can copy the framework; harder to unseat a named category

**2. Network effect**
- Once customers use "Platform-Mediated Gig," they think in that category
- Switching to competitor's different taxonomy creates friction

**3. Institutional standardization path**
- Credit bureaus could adopt: "Income Type: Platform-Mediated Gig"
- Regulators could standardize on: "Report income using archetype classification"
- Becomes de facto standard faster than methodology

**4. Easier to integrate**
- Companies can integrate a taxonomy (9 archetypes) easier than a methodology
- Adoption requires: name the archetype, not learn the methodology

---

## Verdict on Archetype Moat Strength: **YES, Stronger than Decision Relevance Analysis™**

**Evidence:**
- Archetypes are easier to replicate (4-8 weeks vs 8-12 weeks)
- But harder to dislodge (naming creates stickiness)
- Institutional adoption path is clearer and faster
- Network effects support longer-term defensibility

**Moat strength comparison:**
- Decision Relevance Analysis™: Medium-strength moat (methodology-based)
- Archetypes: Strong-strength moat (naming-based) IF standardized

**Conclusion: Archetypes create a stronger, faster-to-adopt moat than Decision Relevance Analysis™.**
