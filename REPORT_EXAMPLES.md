What it is:
  Your largest client represents 80% of your income.

  If they end the engagement—for any reason—you lose $80 of
  every $100. Your small retainer client alone can't replace
  that overnight. You'd need to find $X,000 in new work in
  weeks, not months.
  
What happens if it occurs:
  Your score would drop from 61 (Established Stability) to

  • Plan long-term
  • Invest in your business
  • Feel secure
  
  You'd enter crisis mode: immediate need for new business.

  work in-house) or never (if it's a 5-year relationship).
  But the risk exists as long as this concentration persists.

  The #1 action to improve your stability is to add one
  mid-to-large client ($15K–30K/year). This alone would:
  • Drop concentration from 80% → 60%

PHASE 1: CONCENTRATION REDUCTION (Months 1–6)
──────────────────────────────────────────────
Primary action: Add one mid-sized client (target: 20–25% of income)

Steps:
1. Identify 5–10 companies in your target market (Month 1)
   - Use LinkedIn, industry lists, or warm introductions
   - Look for companies 50–200 people (mid-market sweet spot)

2. Reach out with a specific offer (Month 2)
   - Not "let's talk about my services"
   - Rather: "I'm expanding to help [industry] with [problem]
     and I've solved this 6x in the past"

3. Close initial project (Month 3–4)
   - $5K–10K project or 6-month retainer
   - Use this as proof point

4. Grow to 15–20% of revenue (Month 5–6)
   - Add scope, or add a sibling project
   - Goal: This client is now meaningful

Expected impact: Concentration drops 80% → 65% (+4 pts)


PHASE 2: RECURRING REVENUE BUILD (Months 3–9)
───────────────────────────────────────────────
Secondary action: Convert largest client projects to retainer

Steps:
1. Propose a retainer (Month 3–4)
   - Ask: "What if we moved this to [retainer model]?"
   - Benefit: Predictable budget for them; predictable work for you

2. Structure the retainer (Month 4)
   - 20 hours/month at $X/hour = stable revenue stream
   - Or: "Unlimited ad-hoc, capped at 30 hours/month" (their preference)

3. Grow the retainer over time (Month 5–9)
   - Add team members or scope
   - Goal: $15K–25K/year retainer

Expected impact: Persistence rises 20% → 35% (+2 pts)


PHASE 3: FORWARD VISIBILITY EXTENSION (Months 6–12)
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Tertiary action: Lock in 6–8 month contracts instead of 3–5

Steps:
1. Ask existing clients for longer terms (Month 6–7)
   - Existing clients: "Can we extend our commitment to 8 months?"
   - New clients: Always propose 6-month minimums

2. Bundle projects (Month 8–9)
   - Instead of Q2 project + Q3 project: "6-month engagement"
   - Same work, better contract terms

Expected impact: Forward visibility rises 33% → 50% (+3 pts)

## Usage Notes for Each Audience

### Individual Report
- Print this for the client's personal records
- Use in 1:1 advisory discussions to explain score
- Base your planning conversation around the roadmap
- Reference scenarios to show "what could happen"

### Advisor Report
- Use as **preparation** before client meeting
- Reference during conversation to move quickly
- Use "Conversation Levers" to guide discussion (what could improve score?)
- Note "Hidden Unlock" to explain when constraint priorities should shift
- Plan follow-up actions using "Recommended Actions for Discussion"

### Organization Report
- Import into risk management system
- Use for cohort comparison and benchmarking
- Flag governance thresholds (RED: score <30; YELLOW: constraint active; GREEN: monitor)
- Track trends over time (same client, reassessed quarterly)
- Identify patterns across portfolio (which constraints are common? which are rare?)
