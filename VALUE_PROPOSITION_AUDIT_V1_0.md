## PART 2: VALUE SOURCE ANALYSIS

### For Measurement Standard Products

**What creates value?**

1. **Standardization**
   - Everyone uses the same scale
   - Makes assessments comparable
   - Creates market efficiency
   - Enables institutional trust

2. **Classification**
   - "Where do I sit?" becomes answerable
   - Enables self-understanding
   - Enables comparison to others
   - Provides framework for action

3. **Authority**
   - Independent evaluation (not from decision-maker)
   - Professional credibility
   - Institutional backing
   - Auditability

4. **Documentation**
   - Creates reference document
   - Enables sharing with professionals
   - Creates record over time
   - Enables tracking changes

5. **Comparability**
   - Across time ("I was CPE two years ago; now CPM")
   - Across situations ("Different decisions scored differently")
   - Across institutions ("Lenders use the same scale")
   - Across individuals ("I scored CPL; peers typically CPM")

---

### Why Would Someone Pay?

**Consumers pay for measurement standards because:**
- ✅ They want to know where they sit
- ✅ They want an independent, objective assessment
- ✅ They want something to share with professionals
- ✅ They want to understand if their situation is typical/unusual
- ✅ They want a documented baseline to reference
- ✅ They want common language with advisors

**Not because**: They expect to discover something shocking

---

### Why Would Someone NOT Pay?

**Consumers wouldn't pay if:**
- ❌ They don't need classification
- ❌ They already have an equivalent framework
- ❌ They don't need documentation
- ❌ The measurement isn't authoritative
- ❌ The measurement isn't comparable/standardized
- ❌ The measurement is subjective or arbitrary

---

### Value Differs Between Consumers and Professionals

**Consumer value drivers**:
1. Classification ("Where do I sit?")
2. Understanding ("What does this mean for my situation?")
3. Documentation ("I want something to reference")
4. Validation ("Am I seeing this correctly?")
5. Communication ("I need to discuss this with my advisor")

**Professional value drivers**:
1. Standardization ("We can use this with all clients")
2. Comparability ("We can compare situations")
3. Communication ("Common language with clients")
4. Documentation ("For file documentation")
5. Consistency ("Repeatable, auditable assessment")

Professional value is driven by standardization/systematization.
Consumer value is driven by classification/understanding.

Both value the standardization aspect, but for different reasons.

## PART 3: FICO ANALOGY AUDIT

### FICO Score

**Does FICO create value through insight/surprise?**
- No. Most people aren't surprised by their FICO score.
- A 620 score isn't shocking; people understand why it's low.
- A 780 score isn't surprising; people know they have good credit.
- Yet FICO is worth ~$1 billion annually.

**What creates FICO's value?**
- ✅ Standardization (everyone uses the same scale)
- ✅ Classification (here is your score)
- ✅ Authority (Fair Isaac is the standard)
- ✅ Comparability (across time, across lenders)
- ✅ Documentation (you can share your score)
- ✅ Institutional adoption (lenders require it)

**Lenders don't buy FICO scores to discover insights.** They buy them because:
- Standardized measure enables comparisons
- Authoritative measurement reduces due diligence
- Comparable across all applicants
