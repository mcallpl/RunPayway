## PART 1: CUSTOMER PSYCHOLOGY ANALYSIS

**What is the customer afraid of?**
- "What if I lose my job and can't pay the mortgage?"
- "What if my income drops?"
- "What if interest rates change and I can't refinance?"
- "What if the real estate market crashes?"
- "Am I overextending myself?"

**Uncertainty magnitude**: VERY HIGH (mortgage is biggest decision for most people)

**Anxiety source**: Income sustainability (if employed) or income continuation (if self-employed)

**What does customer wish they knew?**
- "Exactly how much my ability to pay depends on things continuing"
- "Would I be okay if X changed?"
- "How my situation compares to others"

**Current coping mechanisms:**
- Mortgage pre-approval from lender ("Yes, you're approved")
- DTI calculation ("You can afford this")
- Spouse/family discussion
- Financial advisor consultation
- Gut feeling

- They're already checking affordability
- Decision Check would add "what am I depending on?" layer

**Purchase motivation**: Formalize anxiety into measurement ("Let me see this in black and white")

## PART 8: STRONGEST CASE FOR PURCHASE

### Why Someone Should Buy Decision Check™

**Written from pure consumer psychology, not marketing:**

---

You're about to make the biggest financial decision of your life.

You've done your homework:
- Checked your savings
- Calculated your runway
- Got pre-approved by the lender
- Talked to your spouse
- Researched online

And you still have a nagging anxiety: "What am I missing? What am I depending on that I haven't thought about?"

Decision Check forces you to articulate that anxiety into specifics.

It doesn't tell you yes or no. (You didn't ask for that.)

It tells you: "Here's what this decision depends on. Here's how your situation compares to others making similar decisions. Here's what could change that would threaten it."

It gives you specific language for the thing you've been worrying about.

You could talk to an advisor, but that's $200-400 and takes weeks.

You could build a spreadsheet, but that's incomplete and you're already tired of thinking about this.

You could proceed with your gut feeling, but that's what's making you anxious.

Decision Check costs $29 and gives you professional validation that you're thinking about this correctly—or it tells you what you're overlooking.

For a $300k-$500k decision, knowing what you depend on is worth $29.

That's not because we're selling you something.

That's because anxiety about dependency is real, and professional clarity has value.

## PART 9: STRONGEST CASE AGAINST PURCHASE

### Why Nobody Should Buy Decision Check™

**Written with full ruthlessness:**

---

You don't need this.

If you're buying a home: Your lender already assessed whether you can pay. They use sophisticated underwriting. They're betting their money on your ability to continue. That's better validation than some report.

If you're starting a business: You've already done a business plan. You've already calculated runway. You already know what could go wrong because you've been obsessing about it for months. A report won't change that.

If you're retiring: Your financial advisor already did retirement projections. They already stress-tested scenarios. They already know about market risk and longevity risk. They're getting paid to think about this. Why pay another company to tell you the same thing?

If you're changing careers: You've already talked to people who made the transition. You've already calculated your savings. You already know if you can afford the risk. A report won't make you more confident; it'll just give you another thing to worry about.

You also don't want to pay for this.

It's $29. That's not a lot. But psychologically, you're paying for a report that tells you what you're anxious about—in black and white.

You don't actually want to see that in black and white. You want someone to tell you it's going to be fine.

A report can't do that. It can only confirm your anxiety.

So you'll read it, feel worse, and resent the $29.

You also don't trust this.

You've never heard of this company.

You don't know if they're using real methodology or made-up framework.
