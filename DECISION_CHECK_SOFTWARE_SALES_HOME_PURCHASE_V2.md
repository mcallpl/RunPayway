### Structural Implications for Home Purchase

**Structural Constraint: Qualifying Income Gap**

Your stated income ($145k) structurally differs from qualifying income because commission and base qualify under different institutional rules.

Historical institutional analysis shows:
- Base salary: qualifies at 100% (documented, guaranteed, W-2)
- Commission: qualifies at 50-75% of 24-month average (performance-based, documented, but reduced)

Your specific structure creates a qualifying income gap. If your $29k commission averages historically to $29k, institutional standard is to use $14.5k-$21.75k for debt service qualification.

**Structural Reality:** Your $145k stated income structurally produces $130.5k-$137.75k qualifying income. This $7.25k-$14.5k gap (5-10% reduction) is material for debt service capacity.

For a $200k home purchase at 3.5% down, this gap could mean $50k-$100k difference in home price capacity.

**Structural Implication:** You structurally face qualifying income reduction that single-base-salary earners do not. This is not a rejection factor; it's a structural constraint of your income type.

**Structural Advantage: Protected Debt Service Floor**

Your income structure creates a critical advantage for debt service: your base alone ($116k) can support mortgage payment independent of commission performance.

Structural analysis: If your commission declines 50% (drops to $14.5k), your income becomes $130.5k. Your $116k base alone supports this. For debt service, this means your mortgage payment is protected against performance decline up to 50% commission reduction.

Most single-income earners lack this structural protection. Employment loss affects 100% of income immediately. Your structure creates a buffer: commission could decline substantially before employment loss becomes debt service threat.

**Structural Implication:** Your debt service capacity is more resilient than base-salary-only earners because your base-to-commission ratio creates a structural floor. A 30-50% commission decline doesn't threaten debt service the way employment loss would for others.

**Structural Constraint: Single-Employer Inflection**

Your income structure creates a critical dependency: career change or employer change causes immediate structural income reconfiguration.

Analysis: If you change employers or roles within employer, your new compensation structure will be different. New employer might offer: 70% base / 30% commission, or 50% base / 50% commission, or 100% base / 0% commission. The structural composition changes.

For home purchase debt service, this matters because: qualification assumes income structure continues. Structural change (new role, new employer, new compensation model) means re-qualification required during mortgage term.

Most traditional W-2 earners face this differently: employer change affects income level but not income structure. Your change likely affects both.

**Structural Implication:** Your income structure is more sensitive to career decisions than traditional employment structures. Career change that maintains similar total compensation ($140-150k) but changes base/commission ratio could require re-qualification.
