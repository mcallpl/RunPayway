## PART 9: TRANSLATION FAILURE ANALYSIS

### Terms That Should NEVER Appear Above the Fold

**These must be removed from landing page:**

1. ❌ **"Commitment Pressure"**
   - Unexplained technical term
   - Confuses without definition
   - Should appear only: In final report, explained in documentation

2. ❌ **"Support Structure"**
   - Internal framework language
   - Consumers don't think in these terms
   - Should appear only: In documentation, explained in full context

3. ❌ **"Archetype"**
   - Technical classification term
   - Meaningless to consumers without framework knowledge
   - Should appear only: In report, with plain-language translation

4. ❌ **"Gap Analysis"**
   - Technical methodology language
   - Confuses instead of clarifies
   - Should appear only: In methodology documentation, not marketing

5. ❌ **"Financial Commitment Dependence"**
   - Too technical, too long, too confusing
   - Should appear only: In metadata, internal documentation
   - Consumer version: "Decision dependence"

6. ❌ **"Classification System"**
   - Technical language
   - Consumers don't care about system; they care about their result
   - Should appear only: In technical documentation

---

### Where These Terms Should Appear Instead

**Consumer-facing (Landing Page):**
- Remove all technical terms
- Use: "Understand what your decision depends on"

**Professional-facing (Advisor Materials):**
- Can use "Commitment Pressure" (they understand frameworks)
- Explain methodology (they need to understand)

**Technical Documentation:**
- Use full terminology
- Explain architecture for professionals/engineers

**Final Report:**
- Introduce technical terms but translate them
- "We classify your situation as Moderate Commitment Pressure (meaning: your decision depends on several conditions staying the same)"

## SUMMARY: TRANSLATION PRINCIPLES

**Principle 1: Lead with consumer benefit, not framework**
- ❌ "Our Commitment Pressure framework measures..."
- ✅ "Understand what your decision depends on..."

**Principle 2: Use "what" not "how"**
- ❌ "Using gap analysis and archetype classification..."
- ✅ "Shows what conditions your decision requires..."

**Principle 3: Translate technical terms to consumer language**
- ❌ "Commitment Pressure" (first mention)
- ✅ "How dependent your decision is" (then introduce CP as classification)

**Principle 4: Action-oriented language**
- ❌ "We measure financial commitment dependence"
- ✅ "Understand what your decision depends on"

**Principle 5: Specificity before abstraction**
- Lead with specific use cases (home purchase, career change)
- Then generalize (any major financial decision)

**Principle 6: Credibility through clarity, not jargon**
- ❌ "Proprietary gap-analysis framework"
- ✅ "Professional assessment"
