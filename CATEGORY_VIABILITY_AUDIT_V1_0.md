# Financial Commitment Dependence Category Audit™ v1.0

**Status**: STRATEGIC ANALYSIS  
**Date**: June 20, 2026  
**Purpose**: Determine whether "financial commitment dependence" deserves to exist as a standalone measurement category  

---

## CRITICAL QUESTION

Is **Commitment Pressure™** (measurement of how heavily a financial decision depends on supporting conditions remaining intact) important enough to justify a standalone measurement category?

Or is it simply repackaging existing risk assessment with new terminology?

---

## PART 1: EXISTING MEASUREMENT LANDSCAPE

### FICO Score

**What it measures:**
- Creditworthiness based on credit history
- Ability to repay debt historically

**What it fails to measure:**
- ❌ Current income
- ❌ Assets/reserves
- ❌ Employment stability
- ❌ Concentration risk
- ❌ Dependence on conditions
- ❌ Ability to weather disruption

**Does it measure dependence?** NO

---

### Credit Reports

- Collections/judgments

**What it fails to measure:**
- ❌ Income level
- ❌ Income stability

- ❌ Job security
- ❌ Concentration risk
- ❌ Dependence on supporting conditions

**Does it measure dependence?** NO

---

### Debt-to-Income Ratio

**What it measures:**
- Monthly debt payments as percentage of gross income
- Capacity to carry additional debt

**What it fails to measure:**
- ❌ Income stability/variability
- ❌ Income source concentration
- ❌ Duration of income requirement
- ❌ Assets/reserves
- ❌ What must continue to go right
- ❌ Dependence structure

**Does it measure dependence?** PARTIALLY
- DTI measures quantity of income relative to debt
- DTI does NOT measure how fragile that income is
- DTI does NOT measure concentration risk
- DTI does NOT measure duration-specific dependence

---

**What it fails to measure:**

- ❌ Concentration risk

**Does it measure dependence?** NO

## CONCLUSION: MEASUREMENT LANDSCAPE

**Current frameworks explicitly measure:**
- ✅ Creditworthiness (FICO, credit reports)
- ✅ Debt capacity (DTI)
- ✅ Equity position (LTV)
- ✅ Income level (income verification)

- ❌ Structural fragility vs. structural resilience

**Conclusion**: A measurement gap exists.

## PART 2: CATEGORY GAP ANALYSIS

### Can someone have strong financial metrics AND high dependence?

**Scenario A: Software Salesperson (Employed)**

```
Metrics:
- FICO: 750 (strong)
- Income: $150k (strong)
- DTI: 28% (good)
- Assets: $300k (strong)
- Employment: Established, 8 years

BUT:
- Base salary: $80k
- Commission: $70k (highly variable, dependent on quarterly sales cycles)
- Home purchase: $450k (30-year mortgage)
- Monthly payment: $2,400 ($28,800/year = 19% of income)

Dependence analysis:
- If commission drops 50% (recession): DTI becomes 43% (stressed but manageable)
- If commission drops 100% (loss of clients): Cannot service $450k mortgage on $80k salary alone
- Dependence: HIGH on maintaining commission income
- Current frameworks: Show as "qualified" (DTI and assets look good)
```

**Verdict**: YES, someone can have strong metrics and high dependence. ✅

---

### Scenario B: Consultant (Self-Employed)

```
Metrics:
- FICO: 720 (good)
- Income: $120k (appears stable)
- DTI: 35% (acceptable)
- Assets: $150k (acceptable)
- Business: 5 years established

BUT:
- Income varies 40% year-to-year ($72k-$168k range)
- Clients: 8 active clients, largest = 25% of revenue
- Revenue: Project-based, not recurring contracts
- Home purchase: $300k (30-year mortgage)
- Monthly payment: $1,600 ($19,200/year)

Dependence analysis:
- If largest client leaves (30% probability/year): Income = $84k (-30%)
- If recession hits (project delays): Income could drop 50% temporarily
- If health issue requires time off: Income drops to $0 immediately
- Dependence: VERY HIGH on sustained client engagement and health
- Current frameworks: May decline or offer poor terms (variable income is treated as red flag)
```

**Verdict**: YES, someone can have acceptable metrics and very high dependence. ✅

---

### Scenario C: Business Owner (Retirement)

```
Metrics:
- Net worth: $2M (strong)
- Annual income: $120k (good)
- DTI: Not applicable
- FICO: 780 (excellent)
- Assets: Significant

BUT:
- Retirement timeline: 30-40 years
- Income sources: Business revenue + portfolio withdrawal
- Business characteristics: Single-client dependent (70% of revenue)
- Withdrawal strategy: 4% rule ($80k/year)
- Total income need: $120k/year sustained for 30-40 years

Dependence analysis:
- If client leaves: Revenue drops to $36k; total drops to $116k (marginal but stressed)
- If market crashes 40%: Portfolio withdrawal drops to $48k; total = $84k (significant stress)
- If both occur: Income = $84k (cannot sustain planned retirement)
- Dependence: VERY HIGH on both client retention and market recovery
- Current frameworks: Retirement calculator might show this is "sustainable" (portfolio + income), but hidden dependence is critical
```

**Verdict**: YES, strong net worth can mask high dependence on specific conditions. ✅

---

## CRITICAL FINDING

**Multiple scenarios exist where people have strong traditional metrics (FICO, income, assets) but are structurally dependent on specific conditions continuing.**

**These scenarios are currently misclassified or invisible in existing frameworks.**

The measurement gap is real and material.

## PART 5: CATEGORY CREATION ANALYSIS

### How Did Successful Categories Succeed?

**FICO Credit Score**:
- **Problem**: Lenders needed a common language to compare credit risk across institutions
- **Solution**: Standardize creditworthiness into a single number
- **Adoption**: Fannie Mae/Freddie Mac required it → lenders adopted → became standard
- **Value**: Institutional efficiency, comparability, standardization
- **Key insight**: Success came from solving an institutional problem, not a consumer insight

**Credit Reports**:
- **Problem**: Lenders needed standardized information about borrowers
- **Solution**: Gather, compile, standardize credit history
- **Adoption**: Regulatory requirement → institutional adoption
- **Value**: Standardized information, comparability, institutional efficiency
- **Key insight**: Success came from institutional requirement, not consumer demand

**Moody's Ratings**:
- **Problem**: Investors needed a way to compare bond creditworthiness
- **Solution**: Create a standardized rating system
- **Adoption**: Market adoption through institutional investors
- **Value**: Institutional comparability, risk classification
- **Key insight**: Success came from investor need for standardization

**Net Promoter Score**:
- **Problem**: Companies needed a simple metric for customer satisfaction
- **Solution**: Ask "would you recommend?" and create simple metric
- **Adoption**: Corporate adoption through consulting firms and business books
- **Value**: Simple, memorable, actionable
- **Key insight**: Success came from corporate need for a simple metric

---

### Pattern in Category Creation

**Successful categories solve:**
1. **Institutional problems** (comparability, standardization, efficiency)
2. **Information gaps** (professionals need to know something)
3. **Decision problems** (professionals need to make a decision)

**NOT:**
- Consumer insights
- Novel concepts
- Surprising discoveries

**Key insight**: Categories succeed when they standardize something professionals already understand intuitively.

## PART 9: FINAL VERDICT

### Is Financial Commitment Dependence a Legitimate Standalone Measurement Category?

**YES.**

---

### Why Yes?

**1. Measurement gap exists:**
- Current frameworks measure income, assets, creditworthiness, debt capacity
- No current framework explicitly measures "how heavily a decision depends on conditions"
- Multiple real-world scenarios exist where strong metrics mask high dependence
- The gap is material and real

**2. Professionals need it implicitly:**
- CFPs assess dependence through stress testing (implicit)
- Underwriters assess dependence through risk evaluation (implicit)
- Business advisors assess dependence through business planning (implicit)
- But: They do so qualitatively, inconsistently, without standardization

**3. Standardization would add value:**
- Would systematize what professionals assess intuitively
- Would enable comparability across situations
- Would enable documentation and reference
- Would enable professional communication
- Similar to FICO, credit reports, Moody's model

**4. The category is coherent:**
- Well-defined: "How much does X depend on Y continuing?"
- Measurable: Can classify into CPL-CPC levels
- Deterministic: Same conditions = same classification
- Comparable: Can compare across situations and over time

**5. Professional adoption is plausible:**
- Business advisors would likely adopt (medium-high probability)
- CFPs might adopt (medium probability)
- Enterprises might adopt (medium probability)
- Mortgage/lending unlikely (low probability)

---

## FINAL RECOMMENDATION

### ✅ **CATEGORY VIABLE**

**Verdict:** Financial Commitment Dependence is a legitimate, viable measurement category.

1. **Measurement gap is real and material.**
   Multiple scenarios show people with strong traditional metrics but high structural dependence. Current frameworks don't address this.

   Professionals already assess dependence implicitly. Standardization would solve an institutional problem (comparability, consistency, efficiency).

4. **Institutional adoption is plausible.**
   Business advisors and CFPs have incentive to standardize their assessment. Enterprises have incentive to standardize risk frameworks.

   Well-defined, measurable, deterministic, comparable.
