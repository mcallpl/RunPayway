# DECISION CHECK™ REPORT
**Software Sales Engineer | Home Purchase**

---

## YOUR INCOME STRUCTURE

**Annual Income:** $145,000
- Base Salary: $116,000 (80%)
- Commission/Bonus: $29,000 (20%)

**Stability:** Band B (consistent, but variable commission)

---

## KEY INSIGHT #1: YOUR QUALIFYING INCOME

Mortgage lenders use different rules for base salary vs. commission.

**How it works:**
- Your $116k base qualifies at 100%
- Your $29k commission qualifies at 50-75% (because it's performance-based)

**Your situation:** Your qualifying income is approximately **$131k-$138k**, not your stated $145k.

This is normal for sales roles. The 5-10% reduction is typical.

**What this means:** You have less debt service capacity than your stated income suggests. Plan conservatively.

---

## KEY INSIGHT #2: YOUR MORTGAGE SAFETY NET

Your base salary alone ($116k) can support your mortgage payment.

**Example:**
- If your commission drops 50% (down to $14.5k), your income becomes $130.5k
- Your debt service payment doesn't change
- Your mortgage is safe

Compare this to a pure salary earner: any income loss threatens their mortgage immediately. Your structure creates a floor.

**What this means:** Your mortgage is protected against commission decline. Up to 50% commission drop doesn't threaten payment.

---

## KEY INSIGHT #3: CAREER CHANGE MATTERS

If you change jobs or change your compensation structure, your mortgage qualification changes.

**Examples:**

**Scenario A:** New job with 70% base / 30% commission (same $145k total)
- New qualifying income: $115.9k-$125.6k
- You lose $15.4k+ in debt service capacity
- Lenders require re-qualification

**Scenario B:** Internal promotion (increase base, lower commission)
- More base = better qualifying income
- Lenders require re-qualification (income changed)

**Scenario C:** Freelance/contract work
- Qualifying income drops to $72.5k-$108.8k
- Major debt service reduction
- Lenders require re-qualification

**What this means:** If you're planning a career change in the next 2-3 years, timing matters. Your mortgage qualification depends on your current structure.

---

## KEY INSIGHT #4: STRUCTURE FLEXIBILITY

If you're on the edge of mortgage approval, your compensation is flexible.

You can negotiate with your employer: higher base salary in exchange for lower commission (or vice versa).

**Example:**
If lenders approve you for $X but you need $X+$Y more capacity, you could potentially restructure your comp to shift more to base (which qualifies at 100% vs. 50-75% for commission).

**What this means:** You have leverage that pure-salary earners don't have.

---

## KEY INSIGHT #5: WHEN TO APPLY

Apply for your mortgage before major job transitions.

**Why:** Career changes trigger re-qualification. Your structure is favorable now. If you change roles/companies, you might lose that advantage.

**Timeline consideration:**
- Stable in current role → apply whenever ready
- Considering internal promotion → apply before promotion
- Considering external job → apply before job change

**What this means:** Mortgage timing relative to job changes is strategically important.

## BOTTOM LINE

Your income structure is **well-positioned for home purchase**.

**Strengths:**
- Your $116k base is solid, stable employment income
- Your structure creates a debt service safety net
- You have compensation flexibility

**Constraint:**
- Your qualifying income is 5-10% lower than stated
- Career changes trigger re-qualification
- Single employer means employment loss = total income loss

**Decision:** Your structure is favorable. Just plan conservatively around $131k-$138k qualifying income, not $145k.
