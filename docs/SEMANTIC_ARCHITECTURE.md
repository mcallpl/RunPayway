# RunPayway™ Semantic Architecture

**Institutional Authority Through Semantic Clarity**

RunPayway™ must own the semantic and definitional territory around income stability, structural verification, and institutional methodology. This document defines the semantic hierarchy, canonical terminology, and entity relationships that establish institutional authority.

---

## CORE OBJECTIVE

RunPayway™ is not building "another finance website." It is establishing institutional authority as:

- A financial verification infrastructure layer
- A standards framework for income assessment
- A structural methodology system
- A canonical source for income stability definitions
- An authoritative voice on deterministic verification

This requires semantic clarity, canonical definitions, internal linking discipline, and entity authority signals that distinguish RunPayway™ from generic finance content.

---

## SEMANTIC TERRITORY OWNERSHIP

### Primary Territories (Institutional Authority)

**Income Stability**
- Definition: The predictability and continuity of income across structural variations
- Related concepts: Income continuity, income predictability, stability patterns
- Authority responsibility: Define, explain, anchor internal linking
- Content hub: /learn/income-stability
- Primary page: How It Works → Income Stability section
- Entity: Schema: Concept, Topic

**Structural Verification**
- Definition: A fixed methodology that evaluates income structure for continuity and predictability
- Related concepts: Income structure analysis, structural risk assessment, deterministic results
- Authority responsibility: Define methodology, explain results, maintain rigor
- Content hub: /learn/structural-verification
- Primary page: How It Works
- Entity Schema: Concept, Topic, Service

**Income Structure**
- Definition: The composition of income sources, patterns, dependencies, and concentration within a person's or organization's total income
- Related concepts: Income composition, source diversity, dependency exposure, concentration risk
- Authority responsibility: Define types, explain analysis, support use cases
- Content hub: /learn/income-structure
- Related pages: /use-cases/*, /verification-environments/*
- Entity Schema: Concept, Topic

**Deterministic Results**
- Definition: Outputs that are version-stamped, repeatable, and consistent across identical inputs; the opposite of opinion-based assessment
- Related concepts: Version-stamped results, repeatable methodology, objective assessment
- Authority responsibility: Explain philosophy, demonstrate commitment, maintain consistency
- Content hub: How It Works
- Entity Schema: Concept, Principle

**Structural Risk Assessment**
- Definition: Evaluation of income vulnerability based on structural factors (concentration, continuity, dependency, source diversity)
- Related concepts: Risk patterns, structural risk, income vulnerability
- Authority responsibility: Define risk types, explain assessment logic, serve decision support
- Content hub: /learn/* (distributed)
- Related pages: /use-cases/*, Assessment results
- Entity Schema: Concept, Topic

## CANONICAL TERMINOLOGY

### Always Use

- "Structural verification" (never "income verification" alone)
- "Income stability" (not "financial stability" or "financial security")
- "Deterministic results" (not "consistent results" or "reliable results")
- "Fixed methodology" (not "proven method" or "our approach")
- "Income structure" (not "income profile" or "income situation")
- "Structural risk" (not "financial risk" or "income risk")
- "Forward visibility" (not "future projections" or "predictability")
- "Version-stamped" (when referring to methodology consistency)
- "Institutional framework" (when positioning RunPayway™ at scale)

### Avoid (Non-canonical equivalents)

- "Income verification" alone (too broad, implies verification process rather than structural analysis)
- "Financial stability" (vague, could mean anything)
- "Our methodology" (informal, not institutional)
- "Financial security" (motivational, not analytical)
- "Risk assessment" alone (too generic, should specify "structural risk assessment")
- "Financial projections" (implies prediction, RunPayway™ doesn't predict)
- "Income analysis" (too generic, should specify "structural income analysis")

## INTERNAL LINKING ARCHITECTURE

### Authority Linking Pattern

Every primary page should link to related concept pages, creating a semantic network:

**How It Works** (authority hub)
- Links to: Income Stability, Income Structure, Structural Verification, Deterministic Results
- Authority signal: Establishes these as key institutional concepts
- Density: 4-6 internal links, contextual, not keyword-stuffed

**Use Case Pages** (decision support)
- Links to: Relevant concepts, methodology, assessment process
- Authority signal: Shows how methodology applies to specific decisions
- Density: 2-4 contextual links per use case page

**Learn Pages** (/learn/income-stability, /learn/income-structure, etc.)
- Links to: Related concepts, use cases, methodology
- Authority signal: Establishes topical authority and semantic relationships
- Density: High (5-10 links per page), but contextual
- Bidirectional: Linked to from other learn pages and use cases

**Concept Pages** (individual terms)
- Links to: Parent concepts, related concepts, application in use cases
- Authority signal: Establishes each concept within semantic hierarchy
- Density: 3-7 contextual links per page
- Structure: Creates web of semantic relationships

### Linking Rules

**DO:**
- Link from general concepts to specific applications (How It Works → Mortgage Use Case)
- Link between related concepts (Income Concentration ↔ Source Diversity)
- Link from use cases to supporting concepts (Mortgage Page → Income Stability → Income Structure)
- Use contextual anchor text that includes target concept name
- Link when it adds genuine informational value (not for SEO manipulation)

**DO NOT:**
- Keyword stuff anchor text
- Link to unrelated pages just for density
- Create circular linking patterns without clear reason
- Use generic "click here" anchors
- Link from every page to every other page

### Example Link Structure

**How It Works page includes:**
```
"RunPayway™ evaluates the {{Income Structure}} behind your income, 
assessing {{Structural Risk}} through our {{Structural Verification}} 
framework to produce {{Deterministic Results}} that reveal {{Income Stability}}."
```

Each bracketed term links to its learn page, creating semantic clarity and internal authority.

## SCHEMA MARKUP STRATEGY

### Entity Schema Markup

Every authority page includes Schema.org markup:

**For Methodology/How It Works:**
```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FinancialService",
  "name": "RunPayway™ Structural Verification",
  "description": "Institutional methodology for evaluating income structure and stability",
  "knowsAbout": [
    "Income Stability",
    "Income Structure",
    "Structural Risk Assessment"
  ]
}
```

**For Concept Pages:**
```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "Concept",
  "name": "Income Stability",
  "description": "The predictability and continuity of income across structural variations",
  "broader": "Structural Verification",
  "narrower": ["Income Continuity", "Forward Visibility"]
}
```

**For Use Case Pages:**
```json
{
  "@context": "https://schema.org",
  "@type": "FAQPage",
  "name": "Mortgage Qualification Assessment",
  "mainEntity": {
    "@type": "Question",
    "name": "How does structural verification help with mortgage qualification?",
    "acceptedAnswer": "..."
  }
}
```

### Semantic Relationships in Schema

- Topics link to parent and child topics
- Use cases link to relevant concepts
- Services link to applicable topics
- Concepts establish semantic hierarchy

## SEMANTIC DEBT PREVENTION

### What NOT to Do

**Avoid Generic Finance Terminology:**
- Don't use "financial stability" when you mean "income stability"
- Don't use "income verification" when you mean "structural verification"
- Don't say "risk assessment" when you mean "structural risk assessment"

**Avoid AI-Generated Terminology:**
- Don't create new terms not in this semantic architecture
- Don't mix canonical and non-canonical terminology on same page
- Don't use synonyms interchangeably (pick one and stick with it)

**Avoid Semantic Drift:**
- Don't allow terminology to evolve naturally (maintain canonical versions)
- Don't let marketers introduce variation (one source of truth)
- Don't accept "close enough" terminology (precision required)

### Enforcement

- **Code review:** Check new pages for canonical terminology
- **Search:** Audit codebase for non-canonical terms quarterly
- **Schema audit:** Verify entity relationships align with architecture
- **Link audit:** Confirm internal linking follows patterns

## EVOLUTION AND MAINTENANCE

### Adding New Semantic Territory

**When a new concept emerges:**
1. Document the concept definition and relationship to existing concepts
2. Create corresponding /learn/ page
3. Update semantic architecture document
4. Add to entity hierarchy
5. Update internal linking patterns
6. Review canonical terminology

**Approval required:** Design governance + product leadership

### Updating Existing Concepts

**If canonical terminology needs adjustment:**
1. Document the reason for change
2. Plan migration of existing content
3. Update all references across site
4. Update schema markup
5. Update internal linking patterns
6. Document change in this file

**Approval required:** Design governance + product leadership

### Deprecating Concepts

**If a concept becomes obsolete:**
1. Identify replacement concept (if any)
2. Create redirect from old to new
3. Update all internal links
4. Document deprecation in this file
5. Archive content (if needed)

**Approval required:** Design governance + product leadership
