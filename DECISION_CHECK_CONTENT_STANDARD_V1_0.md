# Decision Check™ Content Standard™ v1.0

**Status**: READY FOR AUDIT  
**Version**: 1.0  
**Date**: June 19, 2026  
**Purpose**: Lock the language governance layer for Decision Check™ reports  

---

## PURPOSE

Language consistency creates institutional credibility.

When customers receive a Decision Check™ report, they should recognize it as professionally authored, measurement-based, and authored by an organization with expertise in financial dependence assessment—not personalized coaching or advisory consulting.

This standard ensures that:
- Every report sounds like RunPayway
- The same measured conditions produce substantially similar narratives
- Professional readers (CFPs, underwriters, CFOs) recognize the report as institutional-grade
- Narratives remain deterministic (reproducible from inputs)
- Category protection is maintained (measurement only, no advice/prediction)
- Language scales across all Decision Types™ and Support Structure Archetypes™

This is not about creativity. This is about consistency.

---

## CONTENT GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLE

**The same measured conditions should produce substantially similar narratives regardless of when, where, or by whom the report is generated.**

Consistency demonstrates:
- Institutional maturity (not AI-generated or random variation)
- Deterministic measurement (same inputs → same story)
- Professional credibility (like Moody's, FICO, credit reporting)
- Reproducibility (auditable narratives)

---

## LOCKED CONTENT SECTIONS

This standard governs language in:

1. **Interpretation Section** (30-60 words, 1-2 sentences)
2. **Primary Drivers Section** (3-4 bullets, 8-12 words per bullet)
3. **Implications Section** (1-2 sentences, 20-35 words)

These sections contain narrative content and require language standardization.

**NOT covered by this standard**:
- Measurement (classification name only)
- Position (placement label only)
- Compared With (fixed structure: Decision Type + Archetype)
- Typical Range (fixed structure: "Most comparable situations fall between...")
- Technical Classification (code only)

## SECTION 1: INTERPRETATION STANDARD

### Purpose

Explain what the Commitment Pressure measurement means structurally.

The customer asks: "What does this measurement mean for my situation?"

The Interpretation answers: "Your measurement means X things must continue to go right for this commitment to remain supported."

### Tone Rules

✅ **Allowed**:
- Objective (fact-based, not evaluative)
- Structural (describes dependence, not quality)
- Measured (grounded in classification level)
- Direct (single clear statement)
- Plain English (no jargon, no codes)

❌ **Prohibited**:
- Evaluative ("good," "bad," "strong," "weak," "risky," "safe")
- Predictive ("will," "likely," "probably," "could happen")
- Advisory ("should," "must," "consider," "need to")
- Subjective ("seems," "appears," "suggests")
- Financial advice language ("suitable," "appropriate," "recommended")

### Structure Rules

**Format**: [Decision Type] [measurement class indicator] [structural dependency statement]

**Sentence count**: 1-2 sentences maximum

**Word count**: 30-60 words minimum and maximum

**Tense**: Present tense (describes current structure, not future)

### Approved Interpretation Templates

#### CPL (Low Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
This [Decision Type] depends on few supporting conditions remaining 
intact. The structure supporting this decision is resilient enough to 
absorb most ordinary changes.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment has low dependence on continued support structure 
conditions. Few critical things must continue to go right.
```

**Template C**:
```
The structure supporting this [Decision Type] is broad enough that 
limited conditions must continue unchanged for the decision to remain 
supported.
```

#### CPM (Moderate Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
This [Decision Type] depends moderately on the continued strength of 
the structure supporting it. Several supporting conditions must remain 
intact.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment relies on multiple supporting conditions continuing 
as currently structured. The dependence is moderate but material.
```

**Template C**:
```
The structure supporting this [Decision Type] has adequate but not 
extensive room for change. Several important conditions must continue 
as expected.
```

#### CPE (Elevated Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
This [Decision Type] relies meaningfully on the continued strength 
of the structure supporting it. Several important supporting 
conditions must remain intact.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment depends on sustained support from the current 
structure. Limited room exists for significant changes in supporting 
conditions.
```

**Template C**:
```
The structure supporting this [Decision Type] has concentrated 
characteristics that create elevated dependence. Multiple important 
conditions must continue to function as expected.
```

#### CPH (High Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
This [Decision Type] depends heavily on the current structure 
remaining stable. Many important supporting conditions must continue 
without significant interruption.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment is materially dependent on specific characteristics 
of the supporting structure persisting. Extended change in key support 
areas would threaten this commitment.
```

**Template C**:
```
The structure supporting this [Decision Type] has critical 
dependencies that limit flexibility. Most key conditions must 
continue to be met.
```

#### CPC (Critical Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
This [Decision Type] is critically dependent on the current structure 
remaining largely unchanged. Most key supporting conditions must 
persist without interruption.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment depends on sustained continuation of specific, 
critical support structures. Significant change in any key area would 
materially threaten this commitment.
```

**Template C**:
```
The structure supporting this [Decision Type] has fundamental 
dependencies that cannot absorb major change. Nearly all key 
conditions must remain stable.
```

### Interpretation Construction Rules

**Rule I1: Use "depends" or "relies" language**
- ✅ "This decision depends on..."
- ✅ "This commitment relies on..."
- ❌ "This is risky because..." (advisory/evaluative)
- ❌ "You need..." (advisory)

**Rule I2: Reference "supporting conditions" or "structure" (never "income quality")**
- ✅ "...the structure supporting this decision..."
- ✅ "...supporting conditions remaining intact..."
- ❌ "...stable income..." (judgment about income quality)
- ❌ "...reliable sources..." (judgment)

**Rule I3: Use numbers only when bounded by the Demand Profile**
- ✅ "...30-year mortgage..." (from Decision Type profile)
- ✅ "...18-36 month runway..." (from Decision Type profile)
- ❌ "...high variability..." (relative judgment)
- ❌ "...unpredictable flow..." (relative judgment)

**Rule I4: Present as structural fact, not prediction**
- ✅ "This commitment relies on sustained income continuation." (structural fact)
- ✅ "The supporting structure has limited room for change." (structural fact)
- ❌ "This may fail if..." (predictive)
- ❌ "There is risk that..." (risk assessment)

**Rule I5: Keep customer out of the statement**
- ✅ "This decision depends on..."
- ❌ "You depend on..." (makes it about the customer, not the structure)
- ❌ "Your income..." (advisory framing)

## SECTION 2: PRIMARY DRIVERS STANDARD

### Purpose

Explain why the customer received this Commitment Pressure classification.

The customer asks: "Why is my commitment at this pressure level?"

Primary Drivers answer: "Because of these four specific structural characteristics."

### Tone Rules

✅ **Allowed**:
- Structural facts (characteristics of the supporting structure)
- Causal language (directly related to CP classification)
- Measurement-based (grounded in archetype characteristics)
- Plain English (accessible to consumers)
- Parallel structure (similar phrasing across all bullets)

❌ **Prohibited**:
- Vague language ("various factors," "multiple considerations," "several things")
- Evaluative adjectives ("stable," "unreliable," "risky," "safe," "weak," "strong")
- Predictive claims ("may," "could," "might," "likely")
- Advice ("should consider," "need to," "must")
- Intensity words ("very," "extremely," "highly") without context

### Structure Rules

**Format**: Bullet points only (3-4 bullets exactly)

**Per-bullet word count**: 8-12 words per bullet

**Sentence structure**: Complete phrases (not fragments), parallel construction

**Punctuation**: Period at end of each bullet

### Approved Driver Library

These drivers are pre-approved and locked. Drivers should be drawn from this library, not created ad-hoc.

#### Concentration-Related Drivers

```
• Support depends on a limited number of sources.
• Support is concentrated within a single primary relationship.
• Multiple income sources are not present in the structure.
• Support relies on continuation of a narrowly-defined income stream.
```

#### Continuity-Related Drivers

```
• Support relies on future performance continuing as expected.
• Support requires sustained continuity over an extended period.
• Support depends on ongoing relationship continuation without interruption.
• Income must continue to flow throughout the commitment duration.
```

#### Variability-Related Drivers

```
• Support flow varies based on external transaction completion.
• Income is not fixed and depends on variable circumstances.
• Support levels fluctuate based on business or market conditions.
• Continuation is not guaranteed by contract or employment agreement.
```

#### Duration-Related Drivers

```
• Long-term horizon (30+ years) requires sustained structure persistence.
• Extended duration (18+ months) magnifies effects of support disruption.
• Commitment timeline requires uninterrupted support structure continuation.
• Time horizon creates extended dependence on stable conditions.
```

#### Flexibility-Related Drivers

```
• Limited flexibility exists if supporting conditions change significantly.
• Limited room exists if income sources experience disruption.
• Structure has minimal capacity to adapt to changed conditions.
• Few alternative support sources would be available if primary source changes.
```

#### Match-Related Drivers

```
• Structure characteristics align strongly with commitment requirements.
• Structure provides adequate characteristics for this commitment type.
• Structure characteristics do not fully align with commitment demands.
• Support characteristics create gaps relative to commitment needs.
```

#### External Dependence Drivers

```
• External conditions may influence ongoing support stability.
• Support depends on business environment or market conditions.
• Support is subject to client or market relationship changes.
• Continuation depends on factors beyond direct personal control.
```

### Driver Assignment Rules

**Rule D1: Select exactly 3-4 drivers**

Do not use fewer than 3. Do not use more than 4.

**Rule D2: Draw from Approved Driver Library**

Do not create new drivers. Use only approved patterns.

**Rule D3: Drivers must explain the CP classification**

If the customer is CPE, the four drivers must explain why CPE, not CPM or CPH.

**Rule D4: Drivers should progress logically**

Typically:
1. Concentration characteristics
2. Continuity characteristics
3. Variability characteristics
4. Duration/flexibility characteristics

**Rule D5: Adjust language only for Decision Type context**

The underlying driver concept stays the same. Only contextual words change.

Example:

```
Base Driver: "Support relies on future performance continuing as expected."

Home Purchase variant: "Income continuation requires ongoing employment."
(Added "income," "employment" for context)

Business Launch variant: "Revenue generation requires consistent client engagement."
(Added "revenue," "client" for context)

Retirement variant: "Income flow must be sustained throughout retirement period."
(Added "income," "retirement period" for context)
```

The core concept remains: "Future performance must continue." Only Decision Type context words change.

## SECTION 3: IMPLICATION STANDARD

### Purpose

Explain why the Commitment Pressure matters.

The customer asks: "What does this measurement mean for me?"

Implications answer: "It means this number of important things must continue to go right."

### Tone Rules

✅ **Allowed**:
- Consequence-based (what must remain true)
- Structural (describes ongoing conditions)
- Direct (single clear statement)
- Plain English (accessible language)
- Positive frame (what must continue, not what could fail)

❌ **Prohibited**:
- Prescriptive ("you should," "you need to," "consider")
- Risk-based ("risk," "danger," "threat," "could fail")
- Evaluative ("good," "bad," "strong," "weak")
- Predictive ("will," "may," "likely," "could")

### Structure Rules

**Format**: Sentence statement (no bullets)

**Sentence count**: 1-2 sentences maximum

**Word count**: 20-35 words

**Tense**: Present tense

### Approved Implication Templates

#### CPL (Low Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
Few important things must continue to go right for this commitment 
to remain supported.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment has flexibility to absorb ordinary changes in 
supporting conditions.
```

#### CPM (Moderate Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
Several important things must continue to go right for this 
commitment to remain supported.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment depends on continued stability in several key 
supporting areas.
```

#### CPE (Elevated Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
Several important things must continue to go right for this 
commitment to remain supported.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment requires ongoing stability across multiple key 
supporting areas to function as expected.
```

#### CPH (High Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
Many important things must continue to go right for this commitment 
to remain supported.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment depends on sustained stability across multiple critical 
supporting conditions to function.
```

#### CPC (Critical Commitment Pressure)

**Template A**:
```
Most key things must continue to go right for this commitment to 
remain supported.
```

**Template B**:
```
This commitment depends on sustained stability of nearly all key 
supporting conditions to persist.
```

### Implication Construction Rules

**Rule IM1: Use "must continue to go right" or "must remain intact" language**
- ✅ "Several important things must continue to go right..."
- ✅ "This depends on conditions remaining intact..."
- ❌ "There is risk that..." (risk-based)
- ❌ "You should monitor..." (prescriptive)

**Rule IM2: Connect directly to CP level**
- CPL: "Few important things..."
- CPM: "Several important things..."
- CPE: "Several important things..." (note: CPE and CPM can share implications language)
- CPH: "Many important things..."
- CPC: "Most key things..."

**Rule IM3: One implication per CP classification**

Do not create multiple implications. One per report.

**Rule IM4: Remain abstract (do not name specific conditions)**
- ✅ "Several important things must continue to go right..."
- ❌ "Income must continue and your job must stay stable..." (over-specific)

The specific conditions are named in Primary Drivers. Implications generalize.

## SECTION 8: CONSUMER UNDERSTANDING AUDIT

### Objective

Verify that customers understand the report without framework knowledge.

### Testing Criteria

#### Clarity Test

**Question**: Can a customer understand the Interpretation in one reading?

✅ **PASS**: Customer can state meaning in own words after one read

❌ **FAIL**: Customer requires multiple readings or interpretation

#### Comprehension Test

**Question**: Does the customer understand what Commitment Pressure means?

✅ **PASS**: Customer restates it as "how much things must keep going right"

❌ **FAIL**: Customer confuses it with risk, affordability, or recommendation

#### Driver Logic Test

**Question**: Do Primary Drivers explain why the measurement is at this level?

✅ **PASS**: Customer can connect drivers to the classification

❌ **FAIL**: Drivers seem disconnected from measurement

#### Implication Logic Test

**Question**: Does Implication follow from Interpretation and Drivers?

✅ **PASS**: Implication feels like natural conclusion from Interpretation + Drivers

❌ **FAIL**: Implication feels like separate statement

## SECTION 10: CATEGORY PROTECTION AUDIT

### Objective

Verify that reports remain measurement-only (never drift toward advisory, prediction, or risk assessment).

### Language Prohibited in All Sections

**Advisory Language**:
- ❌ "should"
- ❌ "need to"
- ❌ "must" (prescriptive — different from "things must go right")
- ❌ "recommend"
- ❌ "consider"
